\section{Threats to Validity}\label{sec:threats}

\paragraph{We test decision rules, not systems.}
The threshold curve applies the acceptance rules of Meta-Harness, AutoDesign
and HCL, re-implemented from their papers, to our null comparisons. The rates we
report are the rates of those rules in our regime, not the rates of those
systems on their benchmarks, where models, tasks and noise differ. For the
fixed-versus-re-derived comparison we follow the procedure that RRSI describes
(a tolerance equal to the spread of repeated evaluations of the base, kept
fixed), not its code, and RRSI's acceptance condition has an additional cost
term that we do not model.

\paragraph{One regime.}
All sweeps come from one harness, one inference node and, for the most part,
small local models with large capability gaps. Heterogeneous noise may be a
feature of this regime: the three hosted tool-calling endpoints audited by \citet{chen2026noisefloor} were nearly deterministic between reruns at temperature 0, and if
every configuration of an evolution is that quiet, a single calibration costs
little. Within our range (1B to 27B) the median noise falls with model size but
its tail does not (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}); we have no data on frontier
models.
Our claim is conditional on heterogeneity, and we report heterogeneity itself
as the first result so that others can check whether their regime has it.

\paragraph{The archive is both the source of noise and the test bed.}
The null distributions are built from the same sweeps on which the rules are
evaluated. We mitigate this in three ways: the per-configuration threshold never
sees the pair it decides (leave-one-pair-out), confidence intervals resample
whole sweeps, and we report results per configuration rather than a single
pooled number. Selection is a related concern: the calibration analysis can use
only sweeps with at least two arms of three or more repetitions (26 of 62), and
those sweeps were run for other purposes (model comparisons, ablations of harness
levers). They are not a random sample of configurations an optimizer would
visit.

\paragraph{Unrecorded configuration as noise.}
Part of what we count as run-to-run noise may be configuration that the sweeps
did not record. The harness began recording its inference engine and version in
late August 2026; earlier sweeps do not, and in a controlled experiment on this
node, changing only the KV-cache type changed the outcome of 82\% of the items.
Node drift (driver updates, memory pressure from other processes) can also enter
between repetitions. All of these inflate the apparent noise of some
configurations. They would affect both rules alike within a pair, but they may
inflate the heterogeneity across configurations that drives our main result.

\paragraph{Estimators and small samples.}
With three repetitions, every estimate of $\hat\delta$ rests on very few pairs,
and the maximum is a high-variance estimator. We checked that a second tail
estimator gives the same result and that equalizing the number of pairs does not
change it, but the absolute rates are tied to $k = 3$ to $5$ repetitions. With
more repetitions both rules would estimate the noise better, and the gap between
them would likely shrink for incumbents whose noise resembles the base's.

\paragraph{The power model.}
We model a true improvement as a uniform shift $e$ of the paired difference.
Real harness changes help some tasks and hurt others, and they may change the
noise of the configuration as well as its mean. The power comparison shows the
direction of the effect under the simplest alternative; it does not estimate the
power of either rule against the changes an optimizer actually proposes.

\paragraph{Exclusions.}
We excluded sweeps that the project had labelled invalid or contaminated by
infrastructure failures, by file name, before running the analyses reported
here. On the same frozen corpus, including them adds 164 null comparisons and changes no rate of the threshold curve by more than 0.8 points (for example, 25.5\% instead of 26.3\% for the best-score rule). The exclusion rule and the frozen
manifest are part of the replication package.
