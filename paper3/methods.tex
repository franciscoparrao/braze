\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{The archive}\label{sec:archive}
All data come from the sweep archive of a single agentic coding harness, written
in Rust and evaluated with its own benchmark runner. A sweep runs one or more
\emph{arms} (a model backend plus a harness configuration) over a task suite,
repeating every task $k$ times with fixed seeds. Most sweeps use the project's
two coding suites (a 19-task default suite and a 34-task discriminating suite, 55
of the 62 sweeps that contribute groups): small agentic chores such as reading,
editing and creating files, recovering from tool errors and choosing among
distractor files, graded pass/fail by compilation (\texttt{cargo check}) and
content assertions. The remaining sweeps use a constraint-retention suite under
context compaction, a function-calling anchor derived from BFCL, a verification
suite and a short core suite. The archive was frozen on
2026-10-17 as a manifest of 140 JSON files with their SHA-256 digests; the
analysis scripts verify every digest and abort on any mismatch, so the numbers
in this paper cannot be silently recomputed on a different corpus.

Sweeps that the project itself had marked as invalid or contaminated by
infrastructure failures (out-of-memory kills, transport timeouts, an open
circuit breaker) are excluded by name. Their failures are not model noise, and
an earlier version of our analysis that included them overstated the noise by
162 comparisons. Of the remaining files, 95 contain per-run results. We keep the
158 \emph{groups} (file $\times$ arm) that repeat each of at least eight tasks
at least twice: 9 groups with 2 repetitions, 32 with 3, 112 with 5 and 5 with
10, for 14{,}790 runs and 32 distinct backend specifications. Most are small
models served locally on one node with a 6\,GB GPU (from 1B to 27B total
parameters, through Ollama, llama.cpp in-process or a local OpenAI-compatible
server); three are hosted models reached through an OpenAI-compatible API.

\subsection{Null comparisons}\label{sec:null}
Within a group, two repetitions $a$ and $b$ are two runs of the same
configuration on the same tasks. We treat $b$ as a ``candidate'' and $a$ as the
``incumbent'' and ask whether a decision rule would commit $b$. Since nothing
distinguishes them except sampling, every commit is a false acceptance. We use
both orders of each pair, because noise has no preferred direction, and pair
outcomes by task. For a pair, $\Delta = (P_b - P_a)/n$, where $P$ counts passed
tasks among the $n$ tasks present in both repetitions.

\subsection{Decision rules from published optimizers}\label{sec:rules}
For the threshold curve we re-implement the \emph{decision rule} of each system
as described in its paper, not the system: we criticize rules, not
implementations (Section~\ref{sec:threats}).
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Best score} (Meta-Harness, \citealp{lee2026metaharness}):
  commit if $P_b > P_a$.
  \item \textbf{Train up, dev not down} (AutoDesign,
  \citealp{luo2026autodesign}): split tasks deterministically in halves and
  commit if $b$ improves on the first and does not regress on the second.
  \item \textbf{Margin plus anchor} (HCL, \citealp{kang2026hcl}): commit if $b$
  gains at least $\delta \in \{1, 3\}$ tasks on one half and does not regress on
  the other (the anchor set).
  \item \textbf{Paired exact test}: commit if the exact McNemar test on the
  discordant tasks rejects at $\alpha = 0.05$ in favor of $b$.
\end{itemize}
We draw up to six pairs per group (seed fixed), giving 1{,}614 null comparisons.

\subsection{Fixed versus re-derived tolerance}\label{sec:fixed}
To study calibration we need sweeps with several configurations on the same
suite. We keep the 26 sweeps that contain at least two arms with three or more
repetitions; they hold 149 arms. In each sweep one arm plays the base harness
$H_0$ and the others play incumbents reached later in an evolution.

Both rules commit when $\Delta > \hat\delta$ and differ only in where
$\hat\delta$ comes from:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Fixed} (the RRSI procedure, \citealp{xia2026rrsi}):
  $\hat\delta$ is estimated once from the repeated runs of $H_0$ and applied to
  every incumbent of the sweep.
  \item \textbf{Re-derived per configuration}: $\hat\delta$ is estimated from the
  repeated runs of the incumbent being tested, leaving out the pair under
  evaluation (leave-one-pair-out), so that the threshold never sees the
  comparison it decides.
\end{itemize}
The estimator of $\hat\delta$ is the same on both sides. \citet{xia2026rrsi} define the tolerance as the ``spread of repeated evaluations'' without fixing the statistic, and on one of their domains read it off the first percentile of a half-split null. Our primary analysis operationalizes the spread as the largest $|\Delta|$ among the available pairs. Robustness analyses replace it with
twice the standard deviation of $\Delta$ (another tail estimator) and with the
mean of $|\Delta|$ (a central one). The false-acceptance rate (FAR) of a rule is
the fraction of null pairs of incumbents that it commits; the 90 incumbents
contribute 1{,}814 such pairs.

\paragraph{Choice of $H_0$.} The primary analysis takes the first arm of each
sweep as $H_0$. Because that choice is arbitrary, we repeat the analysis with
$H_0$ drawn at random (200 draws), with the quietest arm, and with the noisiest
arm, where noise is the mean $|\Delta|$ of the arm's pairs.

\paragraph{Number of pairs.} With three repetitions, the leave-one-pair-out
estimate for an incumbent rests on two pairs, while $H_0$ may contribute more.
To rule out that the per-configuration rule wins merely by using fewer pairs,
a variant subsamples the pairs of $H_0$ to the same number.

\paragraph{Uncertainty.} Confidence intervals come from a cluster bootstrap that
resamples whole sweeps (2{,}000 resamples), because pairs within a sweep share a
suite and a node state.

\subsection{Power}\label{sec:power-method}
A rule that never commits has zero false acceptances, so FAR alone cannot rank
rules. To measure power, we add a true improvement $e$ (as a fraction of the
suite) to every null pair of the incumbents, $\Delta' = \Delta + e$, and count the
fraction that each rule commits, for $e \in \{0.03, 0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20\}$.
This preserves the empirical noise of each configuration and shifts only its
mean, which is the alternative a harness change is meant to produce.
